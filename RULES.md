# Bag check — rules as built

## Kit

- 1 detector per player
- 1 conveyor holding 18 security trays
- 18 security trays
- 18 suitcase cards, printed opaque
- 64 item cards, printed on clear stock so only the item shows

44 item cards are permitted. 20 are restricted: knife, bomb, huge bottle of
liquid, poison — five of each.

## Setup

Shuffle the item cards. Deal one under every suitcase card, then keep dealing
the rest at random, no more than five under any one suitcase. Nobody looks.
Close each suitcase, drop it in a tray, load all 18 trays onto the conveyor.

## The shift

There are no turns. Everyone plays at once, off the same belt.

Take the tray at the head of the conveyor and send it through your detector.

**Green.** Nothing restricted inside. Bin the suitcase and keep the tray.

**Red.** At least one restricted item is in there. The detector will not tell
you how many. Now choose:

- **Search it.** Open the suitcase and pull out what's restricted. The item
  cards are clear, so five of them stacked is five drawings on top of each
  other — a knife under a hairbrush is easy to miss. Take what you find, close
  the case, keep the tray. **There is no second scan.** Whatever you left in
  there goes into your pile with the case.
- **Send it back.** Put the whole tray on the tail of the conveyor and take
  the next one instead. It'll come round to somebody eventually.

When the conveyor is empty, the shift is over. Finish the tray in your hands,
then score.

## Scoring

| | |
|---|---|
| Each tray you kept | **+1** |
| Each restricted item you seized | **+2** |
| Each restricted item you let through | **−3** |

Open every suitcase in your pile to find out what you missed. Highest total
wins.

## Two rules this build had to invent

Neither was in the brief. Both were needed to stop the game breaking, and both
are worth arguing about.

**A tray can only be sent back twice.** Without a cap, two officers can bounce
the same nasty suitcase at each other forever and the belt never empties. The
tray carries a marker showing how many times it's been round; on its third
appearance somebody has to deal with it. A cleaner physical version might be
"you can't send back two trays in a row".

**Seizing something permitted costs you nothing but time.** Pull the
sunglasses out and you've wasted seconds you can't get back while the other
lane keeps moving. If real-time pressure turns out not to be enough of a
deterrent, a −1 for a wrongful confiscation is the obvious lever — a passenger
complaint.
